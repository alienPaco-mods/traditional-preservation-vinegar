## 1. What This Mod Does

Vanilla vinegar is loot-only, and without it there's no pickling. This mod lets you brew **malt vinegar** from grain you grow, the old English way: malt the grain, mash it, ferment it into a weak ale, and sour that ale in a cask with a living **Mother of Vinegar**. It ends as ordinary **vanilla Vinegar**, so it works in every jarring recipe.

**One full batch gives about 17 bottles: roughly 85 jars of pickles.**

---

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

**Autumn:** grow barley, wheat or rye → **Steep** → **Malting Floor** → **kiln** on a fire. Malt keeps forever.
**Spring to autumn:** **Mash** → heat → **Strain** → **Fermentation Vessel** (with a packet of yeast) → **Orléans Vinegar Cask** → **Bottle**.

| You need for a full batch | |
|---|---|
| Grain | 48 (about 16 barley plants) |
| Water | 28 L |
| Yeast | 1 full packet |
| Cloth | 1 Ripped Sheet or Cheesecloth (kept) |
| Metal buckets | 2 (returned) |
| Empty bottles | 17 |

## 4. Walkthrough

**Malting.** Steep 24 grain in 4 L of water to make Steeped Grain. Load up to 4 on the **Malting Floor** and press Craft. It sprouts in 5 days (best at 12–20 C). The floor's window says where it stands, the temperature and how fast it is working, with a bar and the time left for each batch. Watch the tray: wet grain, sprouts, matted rootlets, then a malt shovel when done.

**Kilning.** Put Green Malt on a fire or in an oven. It dries into **Malt** in about 3 hours. Don't leave it a day or it burns. **Don't leave it waiting either:** Green Malt is fresh for 2 days and rotten after 5, counted from when the floor finishes. Stale malt still kilns fine, but rotten Green Malt never turns into Malt. Compost it.

**Mashing.** Malt + a metal bucket with exactly 10 L of water gives a **Bucket of Mash**. Heat it 1.5 hours to make **Prepared Mash**, then **Strain Mash** through a cloth. The bucket comes back holding **10 L of Wort**, plus a **Spent Grain**.

**Fermenting.** Right-click the vessel and **Pour Wort**. The vessel's window has a tank for the wort and one for what comes out, and says where it stands and how fast it is working. Put **one full yeast packet** in its slot and press **Craft**. It takes **10 L or 20 L**, in whole buckets only, and gives back 95% as **Fermented Wort** after 10 days. Then **Draw Off** into empty containers, or pump it across with the hose.

**Souring.** Right-click the cask, **Orléans Vinegar Cask**, **Pour Fermented Wort**. Watch the status line: *"19.0 L, 40% soured, about 12 days left."* At 100% it reads **"ready to bottle"**. For the whole picture, just **click the cask** (or pick **Cask Info** in the submenu): bars for how full and how soured, time left, bottles ready, temperature, working rate, the mother and the hose, updating live until you walk away.

**Bottling.** **Bottle Vinegar**, then *Any Bottle*, *All Bottles*, or a specific kind. 1 L per bottle. Leave a few litres in the cask (see below).

## 5. The Orléans Cask

The cask isn't a batch machine. It's a **living pool**, kept going the way French vinegar makers did for centuries.

- It holds **30 L**, but the culture needs air: above **22.5 L** it slows, down to half speed when brimming.
- **New fermented wort thins the pool.** 15 L at 100% plus 5 L of fresh wort is 20 L at 75%.
- **Bottling only works at 100%.**
- **Souring loses about a tenth to the air.** 19 L of fermented wort becomes about 17 L of vinegar.
- **Empty Cask** (in the cask's menu) pours everything away and leaves the mother: for a pool gone wrong, not for every day.
- **Never empty it.** The vinegar you leave behind is the *starter* that powers the mother (next section). Before topping up, keep at least a third as much vinegar in the cask as the fermented wort you're about to add: 6–7 L before a full 19 L batch. Then the mother's speed bonus works from the start.

---

## 6. The Mother of Vinegar

The pale skin that grows on the pool, and the heart of the cask.

- **Grows by itself** the first time a cask reaches 100%.
- **+25% speed** (35 days becomes 26), **but only once the pool is at least 25% soured.** Fresh wort in an empty cask starts slow and speeds up when it passes 25%. Keeping a starter, as above, puts the pool over 25% straight away.
- **Thickens** by a layer for about every 15 L of new vinegar the cask makes after the mother forms, about one full batch. Cask Info shows its layers and how many litres until the next.
- **Take Mother** (empty jar): lift it all out. The cask loses its boost.
- **Split Mother** (2+ layers, empty jar): peel one layer off for a second cask. Until then it's greyed out, and hovering it says how long to go.
- **Add Mother**: put a jarred mother into a cask without one. You get the jar back.
- **A jarred mother is alive.** It's fresh for about 30 days and dead at 40. A fridge slows it.
- **It travels with the cask.** Pick up an empty cask and its mother comes along.

## 7. The Hose

Join the vessel to a cask with a **Rubber Hose**, and pump fermented wort across instead of carrying buckets.

- **Attach Hose to Cask** (vessel's right-click menu): a cask within **2 squares of the vessel**, on the same floor; a Rubber Hose (it comes back when you detach it); pliers (kept). With several casks in reach the menu lists them, nearest first.
- **Pump to Cask** (either station's menu): **5 L**, **10 L**, **Up to the air line** (22.5 L, the fullest the cask sours at full speed) or **All that fits**. Greyed-out options say why.
- Nothing pumps on its own. Top up the Orléans way: bottle some vinegar, then pump in a measured amount.
- **Detach Hose** from either station gives the hose back, and wants the pliers again. Neither station can be picked up while they're joined.

## 9. The Brewer's Almanac (Seasons & Places)

Every station runs on real temperature, **even while you're away**.

| Stage | Best | Slow | Nearly stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Malting Floor | 12–20 C | 5–12 C, above 20 C | below 5 C, 33 C+ |
| Vessel | 18–25 C | 10–18 C, above 30 C | below 10 C |
| Cask | 20–30 C | 10–20 C, above 30 C | below 10 C |

**Where you build matters:**

| Place | Temperature |
|---|---|
| **Open air** | The day's weather |
| **Under an open roof**, or **indoors** | The season: the average of the last 10 days. A room is 22 C while the power grid is on |
| **Loft** (the top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it) | As indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on |
| **Basement** (below ground) | Follows the ground, a month behind the season: about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September |
| **By a fire** (any lit fire within 2 tiles, same floor) | **28 C**, for as long as the fuel lasts; banked embers 24 C |

> *Margin note:* four walls with no roof are still open air. A porch or a lean-to is an open roof; a shed you build with walls and a whole roof is a room.

**First bottles, from the start of malting (two full batches, simulated on the game's weather, the grid off):**

| Start | Open air | Indoors | Loft | Basement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | day 170 | day 182 | day 177 | day 230 |
| April | day 84 | day 92 | day 87 | day 147 |
| July | day 65 | day 53 | day 52 | day 88 |
| October | day 247 | day 261 | day 257 | day 289 |

**By a fire**, any month: the vessel ferments in 11 days and a cask's first batch sours in 35, at 28 C. Banked embers (24 C) are as quick: 10 and 35. Don't malt there: sprouting grain wants it cool, and 28 C takes the floor 14 days instead of 5.

**A powered room** (22 C while the grid is on), any month: the vessel ferments in 10 days and a cask's first batch sours in 35, as by banked embers.

**The brewer's year:** malt in spring or autumn, brew and sour indoors from May to August. A batch started after the summer stalls under 10 C until spring, unless the vessel and cask stand by a fire. A basement is cooler than a room until August, but it holds the summer's warmth into the autumn: a vessel started there in September ferments in 16 to 20 days, while one upstairs can stall for months.

## 10. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **Wort and Fermented Wort are drinkable.** Wort is sweet and filling. Fermented Wort is a flat 4% ale that'll cheer no one but works in a pinch.
- **Spent Grain is animal feed.** Cows, sheep, pigs, chickens and turkeys eat it from a trough. Or compost it. It is fresh 3 days and rotten at 6.
- **Liquids stay pure.** Wort won't mix with other drinks, and Draw Off only offers *empty* containers.
- **Cancel is free.** Cancelling a vessel run returns the wort and the yeast.
- **Chalk X = done.** A finished vessel shows a chalk cross and a sample cup with lees.
- **Bottles pile up at the foot** of a fully soured cask, so you can see a ready cask from across the yard.
- **Skill XP:** Cooking for steeping, mashing, straining, finished vessel runs and bottling; Farming for finished malting runs.
- **Bottles = uses.** One vanilla Vinegar is 10 uses = 1 L. Vanilla jarring takes 2 uses, so **one bottle pickles 5 jars**. The litre is a bookkeeping convention: the bottle keeps vanilla's light weight (0.2), so vinegar is easy to carry once it's bottled. The bulky part is the cask.
- **Leftovers aren't wasted.** Anything under a full litre after bottling stays as starter for the next top-up.
- **Bottles come back.** Use up a Vinegar while pickling and you get its original bottle back, empty (a Water Bottle stays a Water Bottle). Used up any other way, or looted, it leaves an empty Crafted Bottle.

## 11. The Brewer's Notebook

*Torn from the back of an old brewing ledger, with pencil notes in the margin.*

### What's in the bucket

Both liquids are drinkable. Neither is worth brewing just to drink, but in a lean week they'll keep you going. Figures are per litre, with vanilla Beer for comparison:

| Per litre | Wort | Fermented Wort | Vanilla Beer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | 300 | 300 | 500 |
| Carbohydrates | 70 g | 20 g | 36 g |
| Proteins | 2 g | 2 g | 4 g |
| Hunger | -5 | -5 | -10 |
| Thirst | -20 | -20 | -20 |
| Unhappiness | — | +5 | -10 |
| Alcohol | — | 4% | 5% |

A sip scales down: half a litre of wort is 150 calories.

> *Margin note:* why doesn't fermenting cost calories? The yeast eats about 50 g of sugar a litre and leaves about 4% alcohol behind. Alcohol carries 7 calories a gram, so the energy is still in there, just no longer as sugar. 20 g of sugar left plus the alcohol comes back to about 300. Real brewers would nod.

> *Margin note:* fermented wort is ale made without hops. Hops are what keep beer bright and stop it going stale. Unhopped ale tastes flat and sweetish-sour, hence the small hit to your mood.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why malt at all?** Yeast can't eat starch. Sprouting the grain wakes up enzymes that can turn starch into sugar, and kilning stops the sprout before it eats its own store. The hot mash lets those enzymes do their work. Every step in the chain is a real one.
- **Why does green malt spoil?** A sprouted grain keeps growing until it's dried. Left damp on the floor, it grows past its best and then moulds. Real maltsters turned and kilned on a strict schedule for exactly this reason.
- **Why does the cask want air?** The bacteria that make vinegar need oxygen and work only at the surface. That's why Orléans barrels were kept three quarters full, with air holes above the liquid, and why your cask slows past 22.5 L.
- **Why keep a starter?** Fresh ale on its own is easy prey for other microbes. Pour it under sour vinegar and the acid protects it while the mother gets to work. The old rule, "never empty the barrel", is the whole method.
- **What is the mother?** A mat of cellulose spun by the vinegar bacteria themselves. It really does thicken over time, and vinegar makers really did peel layers off to start new barrels.
- **Where does a tenth of it go?** Into the air. Alcohol and a little water evaporate from a barrel that's open to breathe, over weeks of souring.

### Old names, old ways

- English malt vinegar was once called **alegar**, from "ale" and "eager" (sour), just as vinegar comes from the French *vin aigre*, sour wine.
- The **Orléans method** dates to the Middle Ages, when the vinegar makers of Orléans formed their own guild. Barrels were topped up every week or two and never emptied, some for decades.

## 12. Tips

- **Two buckets per run.** One yeast packet either way, and 2 buckets gives twice the vinegar.
- **Malt the whole harvest in autumn.** Malt keeps forever, so brew whenever you like.
- **Hose the vessel to the cask** and pump instead of carrying buckets. **Up to the air line** gives the fastest souring.
- **Before topping up, keep a third as much vinegar in the cask as the wort you're adding.** That's about **6–7 L before a full 19 L batch** (about 21 days to 100% instead of 28) and **3–4 L before a half batch**. Much more than a third overfills the cask, and it slows for lack of air.
- **One cask can keep a household pickling all year.** A second cask (via Split Mother) is for big families or trade.

## 13. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Craft does nothing / says why | Less than 10 L of wort, no full yeast packet, output not drawn off, or already working |
| Yeast refused | It's part-used. A run needs a full packet |
| Can't bottle | The cask isn't 100% soured, or there are no empty 1 L+ bottles nearby |
| Cask stuck at a low % | It's under 10 C. Check the hover tooltip; set the cask within 2 tiles of a lit fire, or wait for summer |
| Pump to Cask greyed out | Hover it: the vessel is empty or short, the cask has no room, or it's already at the air line |
| Can't pick up a station | It holds liquid, is running, or is hosed |

## 15. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPV.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPV.weather()` | The temperature in each place (open air, under an open roof, indoors, a loft, a basement, in a shed, by a fire, by banked embers), and how fast malting, fermenting and souring work there. |
| `TPV.here()` | Which kind of place you're standing in. |
| `TPV.age(hours)` | Gives every floor, vessel and cask this many hours of perfect weather. `TPV.age(120)` finishes a malting run, 240 a vessel run, 840 a cask from fresh. |
| `TPV.fast(hours)` | Test timers: every floor, vessel and cask run takes this many hours, with temperature ignored. `TPV.fast()` turns them off again. It replaces editing `V.TEST_HOURS`. |
| `TPV.timers()` | The hours each stage takes right now, the brewing effort and the temperature sensitivity. |
| `TPV.cask()` | The nearest cask (within 2 squares): litres, souring, bottles ready, mother, layers, litres to the next layer, starter bonus and hose. |
| `TPV.setCask(litres, percent)` | Sets the nearest cask's pool, e.g. `TPV.setCask(16, 100)` for a ready cask. |
| `TPV.mother(layers)` | Gives the nearest cask a mother with that many layers. `TPV.mother(2)` lets you test Split Mother, and `TPV.mother(0)` removes it. |
| `TPV.wort(litres)` | Fills the nearest vessel's Wort tank (20 if you leave the litres out). |
| `TPV.fermented(litres)` | Fills the nearest vessel's Fermented tank, to test drawing off and the hose. |
| `TPV.give(name, n)` | Spawns mod items such as `Malt` or `MotherOfVinegar`, or any full type such as `Base.Yeast` or `Base.RubberHose`. `TPV.give()` lists the names. |

Debug mode also adds **[Debug] Fill with Wort** and **[Debug] Fill with Fermented Wort** to a container's right-click menu.

**Typical check of the hose:** `TPV.give("Base.RubberHose")` and `TPV.give("Base.Pliers")`, attach the hose, then `TPV.fermented(19)` and `TPV.setCask(16, 100)`. Pump to Cask should offer 5 L, 10 L, the air line (6.5 L) and all that fits (14 L).

**Typical check of the mother:** `TPV.setCask(10, 100)`, `TPV.mother(1)`, `TPV.fermented(19)`, pump it all in, then `TPV.age(840)` and `TPV.cask()`. The mother should have grown a second layer.
