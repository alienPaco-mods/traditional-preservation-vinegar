[h1]Traditional Preservation (Vinegar)[/h1]

Make your own malt vinegar from the grain you grow. Malt it, mash it, ferment it with yeast, and let an Orléans cask and its living mother do the rest. It ends in the real vanilla Vinegar, so it works in every vanilla recipe, pickling included. Built for long playthroughs, when the looted vinegar is gone.

[b]Build 42 only (42.20 or newer). This is an early release, so back up your save before enabling it. Singleplayer only: multiplayer is not supported.[/b]

Traditional Preservation (Vinegar) requires [b]Traditional Preservation (Core)[/b] (TP Core for short): subscribe to it too.

[h2]Features[/h2]
[list]
[*]A fully farmable chain from barley, wheat or rye
[*]Ends in the real vanilla Vinegar, for every vanilla recipe
[*]A full batch gives 15 bottles, enough to pickle 75 jars
[*]A buildable Malting Floor, Fermentation Vessel and Orléans Vinegar Cask, each with two facings and its own stages
[*]A continuous cask the Orléans way: draw vinegar off, top up with new, never empty it
[*]A Mother of Vinegar that grows in the cask, speeds it up, and can be split to start another cask
[*]An optional rubber hose to pump fermented wort from the vessel into the cask, in the amount you choose
[*]Temperature matters: a cold winter stops a batch, summer indoors is best, and a fire within 2 tiles keeps it going in winter
[*]Works without power, and keeps working while you are away
[/list]

[h2]The chain[/h2]
[olist]
[*][b]Steep Grain[/b] (crafting menu): 24 barley, wheat or rye seeds and 4 L of water give Steeped Grain.
[*][b]Malting Floor[/b]: load up to 4 Steeped Grain and press Craft. It sprouts into Green Malt in 5 days at 12 to 20 C.
[*][b]Kiln[/b]: put the Green Malt on a fire or in an oven for about 3 hours. Malt keeps forever. Green Malt doesn't: it's fresh for 2 days and rotten after 5, and rotten Green Malt won't kiln. Compost it.
[*][b]Mash Malt[/b] (crafting menu): 1 Malt and a metal bucket holding 10 L of water.
[*][b]Heat the mash[/b] on a fire or in an oven for about 1.5 hours.
[*][b]Strain Mash[/b] (crafting menu, with a Ripped Sheet or Cheesecloth, kept): the bucket comes back holding 10 L of Wort, plus Spent Grain for the composter or your farm animals.
[*][b]Fermentation Vessel[/b]: pour in 10 or 20 L of wort, add one full packet of Yeast and press Craft. 10 days at 18 to 25 C gives 95% back as Fermented Wort.
[*][b]Orléans Vinegar Cask[/b]: pour the fermented wort in. The pool sours in 35 days at 20 to 30 C, 26 with a mother.
[*][b]Bottle Vinegar[/b]: from the cask's right-click menu, 1 L and an empty bottle make one vanilla Vinegar.
[/olist]

From threshed grain, the first vinegar takes about 55 to 60 days in good weather. After that, a cask that's kept topped up gives more every week or so.

[h2]The stations[/h2]
All three are under Furniture in the Build menu and need a hammer (kept).
[list]
[*][b]Malting Floor[/b], Carpentry 2: 6 planks, 8 nails.
[*][b]Fermentation Vessel[/b], Carpentry 3: 8 planks, 12 nails, 4 twine, 1 jar; saw kept.
[*][b]Orléans Vinegar Cask[/b], Carpentry 4: 10 planks, 16 nails, 6 twine, 1 cloth; saw kept.
[/list]

[h2]The vessel[/h2]
The vessel's window has a yeast slot and two tanks, Wort and Fermented. A run takes whole buckets, 10 or 20 L, and one packet of yeast either way, so two buckets is the efficient run. While it ferments the wort is held by the run, and the status line and the batch's hover show how much. Cancel gives back the wort and the yeast. When it's done, Draw Off Fermented Wort into any empty container.

[h2]The Orléans cask[/h2]
The cask is one pool of up to 30 L that you keep going for the whole game. Everything is in its right-click menu.
[list]
[*]New fermented wort thins the pool: 15 L at 100% topped up with 5 L becomes 20 L at 75%.
[*]Souring loses about a fifth to the air, so 19 L of fermented wort becomes about 15.5 L of vinegar.
[*]The culture needs air. Over 22.5 L it slows, to half speed when brimming at 30 L.
[*]You can only bottle once the pool is 100% soured. What's left after the last whole bottle stays behind as starter.
[*]The status line shows the litres, how soured it is and the time left. Click the cask, or pick [b]Cask Info[/b], for a small window with bars for both, the temperature, the working rate, the mother and the hose.
[/list]

[b]The mother.[/b] A cask grows its own Mother of Vinegar the first time it fully sours. It makes souring 25% quicker, but only while the pool is at least 25% soured, so follow the old rule: keep about a third as much vinegar in the cask as the fermented wort you're about to add. Keep much more than that and the cask runs short of air. The mother thickens a layer for about every 15 L of vinegar. From 2 layers you can Split Mother into an empty jar to start another cask, or Take Mother to move it. In a jar it lives about a month.

[b]The hose.[/b] Attach a Rubber Hose (used up, needs pliers) from a vessel to a cask within 2 squares. Then use Pump to Cask on either station: 5 L, 10 L, up to the air line (22.5 L, the best souring speed) or all that fits. Detach gives the hose back.

[h2]Temperature[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Open air[/b]: the day's weather.
[*][b]Under an open roof[/b] or [b]indoors[/b]: an unheated building follows the last 10 days' average outdoors. A room is 22 C while the power grid is on.
[*][b]In a loft[/b] (the top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it): as indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on.
[*][b]In a basement[/b]: follows the ground, a month behind the season: about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September. Warmer than a room in the autumn.
[*][b]By a fire[/b]: any lit fire within 2 tiles holds 28 C (banked embers 24 C): the vessel takes 11 days and a fresh cask 35, in any month.
[/list]
Malt in spring or autumn, brew and sour indoors from May to August, and keep a cask going through winter by the fire. Hover a running batch for its time left and working rate.

[h2]Good to know[/h2]
[list]
[*]Wort and Fermented Wort are real liquids that never mix with anything else. Both are drinkable.
[*]Any empty bottle of 1 L or more works, glass or plastic. A used-up Vinegar gives its bottle back.
[*]Threshed grain doesn't spoil, so one harvest of 48 grain per batch lasts all year.
[*]No vanilla recipe or loot table is changed.
[*]The tiledef number 5130 must be unique among your active mods.
[/list]

[h2]Sandbox options[/h2]
Set the malting, fermenting and souring days, the mother's speed bonus, temperature sensitivity (Off to Harsh) and Brewing effort, which sets how long the kiln, the mash heating and the hand crafts take.

[h2]A little history[/h2]
English malt vinegar, once called alegar, began as soured ale. It was soured by the Orléans method, used in France since the Middle Ages: a barrel kept about three quarters full, with air holes above the liquid and a pale mother floating on top. Every week or two some vinegar was drawn off and the same amount of new ale poured in under the mother. The barrel was never emptied, and a thick mother could have a layer peeled off to start another barrel.
